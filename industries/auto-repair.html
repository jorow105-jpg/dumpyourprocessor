Card processing built for auto repair shops.

A $1,200 repair ticket and a $40 tip get taxed very differently by a flat-rate processor, and most shops are on pricing built for the wrong one. Average tickets in the hundreds to low thousands mean the rate structure you're on matters more here than in almost any other business.

Where the fees hide

  • Flat-rate pricing punishes big tickets — A flat percentage that's fine on a small sale becomes expensive fast once your average repair runs into the hundreds or thousands of dollars.
  • Phone and keyed-in payments — Taking a card number over the phone for an estimate or pickup approval is a keyed transaction — priced higher, and a common spot for surcharges to hide.
  • Deposits on parts and long jobs — Collecting a deposit before ordering parts, then the balance at pickup, means two transactions each carrying their own fee if the setup isn't built for it.
  • Fleet and commercial accounts — Business and fleet cards qualify for lower interchange with the right “level 2/3” data on the transaction — most shops never capture it and overpay as a result.

What to look for

  • Interchange-plus pricing, never flat-rate, for anything above small-ticket volume
  • Invoicing tools for deposits and balance-due payments
  • Level 2/3 data capture for commercial and fleet cards
  • No equipment lease on your counter terminal
